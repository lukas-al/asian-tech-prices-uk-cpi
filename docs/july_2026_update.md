# July 2026 update

Data were refreshed on 24 August 2026. UK CPI and the main ONS technology import
price indices now run through July 2026. Foreign source snapshots were also
refreshed; the targeted five-country contribution remains dated June because
that is the latest common observation across its national series.

## UK border prices

| Import-price measure | July monthly change | July annual change |
|---|---:|---:|
| C26 computers, electronics and optical goods | -1.50% | -1.89% |
| C26 non-EU | -1.98% | -2.59% |
| C261 electronic components and boards | -0.64% | +0.48% |
| C261 non-EU | -1.04% | +1.92% |

The July data weaken the idea that the upstream shock is currently broadening
through UK border prices. C26 prices fell sharply and their annual rates turned
negative. C261 annual inflation remained positive, particularly for non-EU
imports, but it slowed and the monthly rates were negative. This is better read
as residual component pressure than as confirmation of an intensifying import-
price shock. C262 remains stale at September 2025 and is not used as a current
indicator.

## Upstream and retail readings

The common Asian factor remained elevated at 0.95 standard deviations above its
sample mean, but its July innovation was -0.52 standard deviations. Implied
Asian-origin C26 pressure eased from the revised 3.22pp in June to 2.93pp in
July. UK technology CPI also fell in July: the custom headline index declined
1.51% month on month and was 1.36% lower over twelve months; the preferred
ex-games index declined 1.01% on the month and was broadly flat (-0.07%) over
twelve months.

The monitoring conclusion is therefore more cautious than in June: upstream
pressure is still elevated, but July provides no fresh UK-border confirmation
that it is intensifying. Continue to monitor C261, especially non-EU C261, and
require renewed monthly increases before translating the upstream scenario into
forecast judgement.

## Conditional scenarios

The scenarios are not forecasts. Holding the July 2.93pp upstream pressure
constant produces a modelled ex-games headline-CPI contribution of about
0.042pp after six months, 0.080pp after nine months and 0.125pp after twelve
months. An intensification to the historical 95th percentile gives about
0.34pp after twelve months. These estimates remain highly uncertain and should
not override the negative July border-price signal.
